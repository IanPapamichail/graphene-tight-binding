Quantum Mechanics of the Graphene 6-Site Ring

In the Tight-Binding approximation, we model the graphene lattice by assuming electrons are highly localized around carbon nuclei, hopping only to nearest-neighbor atoms. Each carbon atom contributes one electron from its pz orbital perpendicular to the graphene plane.

The Hamiltonian matrix H holds the orbital interaction energy. The diagonal entries Hi,i represent the electrostatic potential energy (on-site energy εi) at each atom. The off-diagonal entries Hi,j represent the overlap integral or kinetic energy cost of tunneling between neighbor atoms (hopping parameter -t).

Solving the time-independent Schrödinger Equation H|ψ⟩ = E|ψ⟩ gives the allowed energy eigenvalues and corresponding eigenstate wavefunctions.

  • Ideal Graphene: When all εi = 0, we get energies [-2t, -1t, -1t, 1t, 1t, 2t]. The degeneracies at ±1t are protected by the C6v symmetry of the ring.
  • Boron Nitride Alternation: Alternating the on-site energies breaks the inversion and sublattice symmetry, opening up an energy gap between valence and conduction bands, representing a transition from a metal to a wide-gap insulator.
